# Explicit-choice rescue trial

Decision: do not advance this version. Of eight new matched triplets, S1 passed three pending, three delegated and two settled drafts through the original route. The rescue produced the correct question on all three eligible pending drafts, but added questions on two delegated drafts and one settled draft. The other 16 fresh drafts took the existing clarification or advisory routes and received no rescue call. This experiment measures additional questions, not the quality of existing discovery.

The frozen fixtures vary ownership of the same alternatives within each family. The five historical S1 misses are a separate regression set. After baseline routing, eligible drafts received one local Granite Q8_0 non-thinking call. Only a pending or incompatible classification with two different literal excerpts, a literal evidence passage, and a nonempty question could trigger rescue. Expected labels and gold alternatives were kept out of model inputs. One evaluator checked the emitted questions against the frozen choices. One run, simulated unknown host, no editor observation.

The rescue recovered the alias migration and proof-method regressions. It proposed relevant questions for ordering, trailer duration and quiz feedback, but the literal-evidence rule rejected paraphrased, noncontiguous or missing alternative fields. Conversely, literal evidence was insufficient on the fresh controls. The model quoted the teacher’s settled dictation choice and still asked which method was preferred. In the audio-tour case the selected evidence established two possible durations but omitted the sentence establishing ownership; the question was nevertheless relevant. No tuning or second attempt was included in these scores.
